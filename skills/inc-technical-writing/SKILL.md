# Technical writing

Use this skill on any document, not just IncPads. Keep the main path short enough to scan. Put supporting evidence, edge cases, and background after the main point or in an appendix. Do not remove details the reader needs to make a decision.

## Draft for the reader

1. Name the audience and the decision or action they need to take. Lead with the purpose, the outcome, and the requested action. If there is no action, state what the reader should understand.
2. Put the product or decision-facing answer before implementation details. Use headings, short paragraphs, and lists that each convey one idea. Move optional depth to a labeled subsection or appendix. In an IncPad, use a collapsed native `<details>` below the section's open summary for optional prose.
3. Write mostly subject-verb-object sentences. Keep each sentence to 30 words or fewer, with one idea per sentence. Split long sentences rather than dropping necessary conditions.
4. Prefer observable facts, quantities, and sources over interpretations. Replace "many users" with a measured number and its denominator when known. Replace "a long delay" with a measured duration when known. Never invent a number to satisfy this rule; if unmeasured, say what is unknown.
5. Delete adverbs, weasel words, and needless qualifiers ("very," "really," "I think," "it seems," "probably") unless uncertainty itself matters. State the uncertainty and what would resolve it. Avoid evaluative adjectives; use concrete descriptions instead.
6. Use plain words for the intended audience. Expand an acronym or define a technical term on first use when a newcomer may not know it. Keep necessary domain terms consistent after defining them.

## Check before sending or publishing

- **So what?** Can the reader say why this exists, what it means for them, and what they should do next?
- **Scan:** Can the reader get the main answer from the opening and headings without reading optional detail? Is supporting material below the answer or in an appendix?
- **Sentences:** Does each sentence carry one idea in 30 words or fewer? Are the subject and action clear?
- **Evidence:** Are quantities grounded in a source? Did vague adjectives, adverbs, opinions, or weasel words replace a fact or hide an unknown?
- **Audience:** Are first-use acronyms and technical terms explained where this reader needs them?

Revise any answer that fails the check, then publish. Do not turn the checklist into a visible boilerplate section in the document.
